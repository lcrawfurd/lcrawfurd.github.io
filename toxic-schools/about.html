Toxic Schools: Proximity to Polluted Sites in 17 Low- and Middle-Income Countries

Lee Crawfurd  ·  Center for Global Development  ·  April 2026
We provide the first cross-country assessment of school proximity to known polluted sites in low- and middle-income countries, combining geocoded data on 2,840 contaminated sites from the Pure Earth Toxic Sites Identification Program (TSIP) with the locations of approximately 2.7 million schools across 17 countries. We find that at least 171,348 schools (6.3%) are within 5 km of a known polluted site — a lower bound, as the TSIP captures only a fraction of actual contaminated sites. Private schools are consistently more likely than public schools to be near polluted sites, a reversal of the pattern found in the United States, driven mainly by the co-location of private schools and industry in wealthy urban areas.

Proximity is a lower bound on true exposure: the TSIP catalogs only a fraction of actual contaminated sites (estimates suggest one-seventh to one-ninth of real sites in Ghana), and the database includes both active and legacy sites. Schools near polluted sites are located in wealthier neighbourhoods in 16 of 17 countries, reflecting the concentration of private schools, industry, and wealth in urban cores rather than land-market sorting by disadvantaged families.

About the map

The interactive map on this site displays all 4,596 TSIP sites globally (coloured by industry type) alongside school locations for the 14 countries covered by Overture Maps in our dataset. School markers cluster at low zoom and expand on zoom-in. Clicking any site marker shows the site name, industry category, and a list of nearby schools with distances.

Note on map coverage: the map shows schools for 14 countries derived from Overture Maps / OpenStreetMap. Five additional countries in the paper (Argentina, Bangladesh, Mexico, Pakistan, Uruguay) use national EMIS data that are not yet included in the map layer. The paper's 6.3% proximity figure covers all 17 countries including these five.

Layers:

  • Polluted sites (TSIP) — all 4,596 sites; circle size scales with number of nearby schools; colour indicates industry type.
  • Schools within 1 km — 15,957 schools (clustered); toggled on by default.
  • Schools 1–5 km — 124,111 additional schools; loaded on demand (~9 MB).
  • 5 km buffer circles — shows the 5 km catchment ring around each site with at least one nearby school.

Data sources

Contaminated sites

Polluted site locations come from the Toxic Sites Identification Program (TSIP) maintained by Pure Earth, downloaded in March 2026 via the contaminatedsites.org API. The TSIP is the most comprehensive global inventory of contaminated sites in lower-income settings. Sites are identified through government requests, community reports, academic research, and media coverage. The database includes both active and inactive legacy sites across a range of pollutants: lead (1,582 sites globally), mercury (620), arsenic (464), chromium (404), and others.

The TSIP substantially undercounts actual contaminated sites — estimates suggest it captures one-seventh to one-ninth of real sites in Ghana. Proximity figures in the paper and map are therefore lower bounds on true school–pollution proximity.

School locations

The paper uses two types of school location data. National school census (EMIS) data were used for 11 countries; Overture Maps data were used for 6 additional countries where EMIS data with geocoordinates are not publicly available. The map on this site uses Overture Maps data for 14 countries (the 6 Overture-only countries plus 8 of the EMIS countries where Overture coverage is also available).

Neighborhood wealth

The paper uses the Meta Relative Wealth Index (RWI) of Chi et al. (2022), which provides gridded estimates of relative household wealth at 2.4 km resolution for all LMICs, derived from machine learning on satellite imagery and connectivity data. RWI data are available from the Humanitarian Data Exchange.
